Source of the Stories

These Bidpai fables, drawn from Maude Barrows Dutton's 1908 collection, offer timeless lessons in morality and wit. The stories feature a rich cast of animals and humans—lions, hares, crows, camels, and kings—who navigate dilemmas of trust, greed, folly, and justice. From the loyal falcon sacrificed by an angry king to the clever hare who tricks a tyrant lion into a well, each tale delivers a pithy insight into human nature. Whether illustrating the perils of imitation, the rewards of courage, or the cost of cruelty, these ancient Indian fables remain delightfully instructive, blending simple storytelling with profound observations on virtue and vice.
