2-3. Ven. Sāriputta and Ven. Moggallāna


high-definition creative commons posters depicting Ven. Sāriputta and Ven. Moggallāna from the Great Chronicles together with further information.

An immeasurable period and 100,000 aeons ago during the era of Buddha Anomadassī, two wealthy friends, Sarada and Sirivaḍḍhana, sought spiritual liberation. Sarada became a mountain hermit with 74,000 followers. Upon meeting Buddha Anomadassī, Sarada held a floral canopy over him for seven days in deep devotion. Inspired by the chief disciple Nisabha, Sarada aspired to become the right-hand chief disciple of a future Buddha. His friend Sirivaḍḍhana similarly aspired to become the left-hand chief disciple. In their final existence under Buddha Gotama, they were reborn as Upatissa (Sāriputta) and Kolita (Moggallāna). Upon hearing the Dhamma from Ven. Assaji, Upatissa attained Stream-entry and shared it with Kolita. They joined the Saṅgha, became Arahats, and were appointed the Buddha’s two Chief Disciples, foremost in wisdom (Paññā) and psychic powers (Iddhi).
