In The Records of a Weather-Exposed Skeleton (Nozarashi Kikō), Matsuo Bashō travels across 17th-century Japan. Departing Edo, he journeys through mist, rain, and mountains—crossing Hakone, Ise, and Yoshino—while reflecting on impermanence, loss, and nature’s quiet power. Reaching his hometown, he mourns his mother’s death over her hair which was preserved. Along the way, Bashō and his companion Chiri compose haiku, interweaving solitude, grief, and beauty.
