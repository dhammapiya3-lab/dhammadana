inspired by the writings of Matsuo Bashō in 17th century Japan

These storybooks are based on the travel diaries and poetic logs of the celebrated Japanese writer Matsuo Bashō, chronicling several of his long-distance pilgrimages. Through a blend of lyrical prose and haiku, the texts document his experiences visiting sacred shrines, historical landmarks, and the homes of fellow poets. The narratives illustrate a deep commitment to nature and a rejection of worldly comforts, as the author adopts the persona of a weather-exposed skeleton or a humble wanderer. Throughout his travels in regions such as Ise, Yoshino, and Kashima, Bashō captures moments of profound spiritual reflection, grief over family loss, and the quiet beauty of the changing seasons. By recording these encounters with both the natural landscape and local people, the sources provide an intimate look at the artistic philosophy and solitary lifestyle of a dedicated poet-monk.
