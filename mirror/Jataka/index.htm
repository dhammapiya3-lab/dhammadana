Source of the Stories

This is a collection of 32 Jātaka tales, ancient Buddhist stories detailing the previous lives of the Buddha that impart moral lessons. They are selected from the first 50 stories in the Pāḷi Jātaka collection. The narratives typically follow a consistent structure: a “story of the present” involving the Buddha’s contemporary disciples, followed by a “story of the past” where the Bodhisatta appears in a different form, such as a merchant or a king of the deer. Key themes throughout the texts include the importance of prudence, caution, and compassion in overcoming worldly dangers and spiritual obstacles. For instance, one tale contrasts a wise caravan leader with a foolish one to emphasize the value of truth over deception. Another story highlights how renunciation and contentment bring a sense of security that royal power cannot provide. The records serve therefore as a guide for practitioners to navigate the path to enlightenment by avoiding the traps laid by the senses and strictly following the Dhamma.
